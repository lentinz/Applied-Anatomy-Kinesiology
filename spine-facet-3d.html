About this model

A schematic motion segment: two bones (and the disc, in the lower spine). The lower vertebra is fixed and the upper vertebra moves. The arrow on each facet shows the direction the upper vertebra's inferior facet glides on the lower vertebra's superior facet.

  • Facet plane angles use typical textbook values: cervical about 45° to the transverse plane; thoracic about 60° (transverse) and 20° (frontal); lumbar about 90° (transverse) and 45° (frontal).
  • Motion ranges are exaggerated so the gliding is visible. Real segmental ranges are smaller, especially lumbar rotation.
  • Cervical side bending and rotation are shown as one coupled, same-side motion. Thoracic and lumbar motions are shown as single-plane motions, although coupling patterns there vary between individuals and studies.
  • The occiput–C1 and C1–C2 models show a simplified skull base and atlas. At the atlanto-axial joint the settling drop is exaggerated, and flexion, extension, and side bending are shown as a rock with no arrow because no roll-glide pairing was sourced.
  • Arrow directions come from the rule text, which follows the course lab key. The bone motion is a simplified rigid-body model and can differ in detail.
  • Individual anatomy varies.
